What is being predicted

The same quasi-2D flume as the experiments: a 1 cm slice of dry sand (1276.3 kg/m³) released at rest on a rigid slope that pivots about a fixed point above a horizontal floor. Each LS-DYNA run solves the release with SPH until the mass settles; the page predicts the final deposit surface, drawn as height above the slope or floor beneath it.

How well it works

Scored on 150 random shapes generated with a different seed and never used for training or model selection:

The light-blue band is the spread of the five ensemble members — their disagreement, not a calibrated probability. It widens where the shape is unlike anything in the training set. Outside the trained ranges (slope 30–60°, 100–3000 particles, thickness up to 36 cm, front between 0.8 and 2.3 m up the slope) the prediction is an extrapolation and the page says so.

Limits worth knowing

Only the final deposit is predicted, not the flow. The underlying model was calibrated against one block size at seven angles; other sizes and shapes are model predictions, not experimentally validated. Below about 35° the calibrated rheology keeps creeping slowly after the main flow stops, so the deposit shape there depends on when the simulation ends (the schedule used is in the repository).
